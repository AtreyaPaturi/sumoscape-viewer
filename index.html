What SUMOscape shows

SUMOscape is a viewer for the SUMO2/3 acceptor lysines of any protein in a site-resolved SUMO proteomics dataset. It opens on the human dataset of Hendriks et al. 2018 ( sites on proteins; control, proteasome inhibition with MG132 and heat shock) and also carries the mouse organ dataset of the same paper; a table of your own can be loaded as a further dataset.

For every protein it draws one lollipop panel per condition — the Z-score of each site in that condition — above tracks with every domain, region, motif, topology feature and lysine modification annotated in UniProt and every Pfam domain from InterPro, each labelled. The Z-score landscape places the protein's sites among all sites of the dataset, the Heatmap compares the conditions site by site, and Structure shows the sites on the AlphaFold model.

Seven steps

  1. Add proteins under Proteins: type a gene or accession, paste a list, or browse all proteins of the dataset. Proteins.
  2. Read the site map. One panel per condition; a stem goes up when the site is higher in that condition than in the others, down when lower. Filled heads are significant (q < 0.05). Site map.
  3. Zoom with the mouse wheel over a protein, drag to move, double click to see it whole. Below about 110 residues the sequence appears on the axis.
  4. Find a site in the table at the right; a click selects it everywhere and shows its details below the table.
  5. Place the sites among all others in the Z-score landscape. How to read it.
  6. See the structure: the AlphaFold model with the sites, coloured as in the site map. Structure.
  7. Share or export: Copy link reproduces the view; SVG, PNG and CSV download what is shown. Export.

Keyboard and mouse

? opens this help, Esc closes it. Over a site map: wheel = zoom, drag = move, double click = whole protein. Every ⓘ opens the help at the entry for the control next to it.

Adding proteins

Type a gene symbol or a UniProt accession; suggestions come from the proteins of the current dataset, with their number of sites. Add (or Enter) adds the protein below the others; any number can be shown at once, each with its own panels and tracks. A click on a chip makes that protein the active one (its sites fill the table, the landscape and the structure); × removes it.

A protein that is not in the dataset can be added too: it is looked up in UniProt (reviewed entry of the dataset's organism), and its domains and lysines are drawn without sites. This is how SUMOscape works for any protein, not only those of one complex.

Browse all proteins

Browse all proteins… lists every protein of the dataset with its number of sites. Filter by gene, accession or name, sort by a column, tick proteins and Add selected, or Add all shown. Up to proteins are added at once so that the page stays responsive; narrow the filter to add a different set.

Paste a list

Paste a list… opens a box for genes or accessions separated by spaces, commas, semicolons or lines (for example a hit list). Ctrl+Enter adds them; names that cannot be resolved are reported.

What the site map shows

Each protein is a block. The horizontal axis is the residue position of the protein's sequence (the accession given by the dataset, which may be an isoform). Above the tracks there is one panel per condition; in each, every site is a stem from the zero line to its Z-score in that condition, with a head at the tip. Up means the site is relatively higher in that condition, down relatively lower.

All panels share the same Z scale, so the heights can be compared between conditions and between proteins. A vertical dashed line marks the selected site through all panels and tracks.

One panel per condition

The earlier SUMO Site Map drew one lollipop for MG132 vs control or heat vs control. SUMOscape draws control, MG132 and heat shock as three separate panels, so that the baseline is seen directly rather than folded into a ratio. The buttons under Conditions shown switch panels on and off; for the mouse dataset there is one panel per organ.

Note that the three Z-scores of a site are not independent: they are standardised across the three conditions for that site, so they add up to zero. A site that is high in MG132 and in heat shock is necessarily low in control. See What a Z-score is here.

Colourings

  • Condition: the colour of the panel's condition; the head is filled when the site is significant in that condition (q < 0.05), open otherwise. Control has no test of its own; its heads are filled when the site is significant in any condition.
  • Z-score: a blue–white–red scale of the Z-score.
  • Motif: the SUMO consensus around the lysine, read off the sequence: ψKxE (ψ = A, I, L, M, F, V or P), other KxE, the inverted motif [ED]xK, or none.
  • Cell lines (human dataset): in how many of HEK293, HeLa and U2OS the site was detected.
  • Known in UniProt: whether UniProt already records a SUMO cross-link at that lysine, another lysine modification (ubiquitin, acetyl, methyl…), or nothing.
  • Domain context: whether the lysine lies in a domain (UniProt or Pfam), in a disordered region, or elsewhere.

A click on a legend entry hides those sites; a second click shows them again.

Site labels

Every site can be labelled with its residue (K524). Auto labels as many as fit without overlap, the strongest first; All labels every site (overlaps possible when zoomed out); Significant only those with q < 0.05 in that panel's condition; None hides them. The selected site is always labelled. Comfortable and Compact set the height of the panels.

Zoom and sequence

Wheel over a protein zooms around the pointer; drag moves; double click returns to the whole protein. When fewer than about 110 residues are in view, the residue letters appear on the axis with lysines in red. Each protein keeps its own zoom.

Why more domains than before

The earlier SUMO Site Map kept seven UniProt feature types and hid every annotation when the accession was an isoform. SUMOscape keeps the further types listed above, adds the Pfam domains, and maps the canonical annotation onto isoforms by their residues, so isoforms show their domains too. A note under the protein's name says when this was done.

Isoforms and changed sequences

UniProt annotates the canonical sequence. When a site table names an isoform, or the canonical sequence has changed since the table was made, each feature is placed on the table's own sequence by searching for its residues (with six flanking residues for short features). A feature whose residues are not found once and only once in the target is left out rather than placed at a wrong position.

How to read it

Every site of the dataset is a grey point at its Z-score in two conditions (by default MG132 on x and heat shock on y). The sites of the proteins shown are drawn on top, in the protein's colour, and labelled. Hovering a grey point names it; a click on a grey point adds its protein.

With MG132 and heat shock as axes, the quadrants read: upper left = higher in heat shock (HS-preferring), upper right = higher in both stresses than in control (both up), lower right = MG132-preferring, lower left = highest in control (both down).

What a Z-score is here

In this dataset the Z-scores are standardised per site across its conditions (Hendriks et al. 2018). For every site Z(control) + Z(MG132) + Z(heat shock) = 0. A Z-score therefore says in which condition a site is relatively high, not by how much it changed: a site that barely changes and one that rises a hundred-fold can have the same Z-scores. For the size of the change use the log2 fold change and the q-value in the details and in the table.

Because control is fixed by the other two, the landscape is complete in two dimensions: the dashed iso-control lines join the points of equal control Z-score (Zctrl = −(x + y)). They are drawn when the two axes are MG132 and heat shock.

Why Z-scores rather than log2 fold changes

Fold changes need a reference condition (control) and their spread differs between conditions; Z-scores put all conditions — control included — on the same scale and so can be drawn as separate, comparable panels and as any pair of axes. The price is that the magnitude of the change is lost, which is why the fold changes and q-values stay in the table and in the details.

The heatmap

One row per site of the proteins shown (grouped by protein, in sequence order), one column per condition, coloured by Z-score on the same scale as the site map. A dot marks q < 0.05 in that condition. A click on a row selects the site.

The structure view

The AlphaFold DB model of the active protein (canonical UniProt sequence), fetched by your browser. The sites are drawn as spheres, coloured by the Z-score of the chosen condition or by any colouring of the site map; the selected site is labelled and in focus. colour protein by → confidence (pLDDT) colours the model by the confidence of the prediction: dark blue ≥ 90, light blue 70–90, yellow 50–70, orange < 50.

A site is placed on the model by its position when the dataset's sequence and the model's are the same, otherwise by its 15-residue sequence context; a site that cannot be placed is listed in the message box. Isoform-only sites have no model.

Which domains carry SUMO sites

colour protein by → domains (UniProt) or Pfam domains paints every annotated domain on the model. A domain that contains at least one of the sites shown gets its own colour and, with labels on, its name and number of sites in the 3D view; a domain without sites is drawn in pale grey, and the rest of the chain in near-white. UniProt domains include repeats, zinc fingers, DNA-binding regions and coiled coils; a domain nested in another is drawn on top.

The legend at the bottom right lists the domains with sites first — range, number of sites and how many of them have q < 0.05 in any condition — then those without. A click on a domain zooms to it; a second click shows the whole model again. Sites hidden through the legend of the site colouring are not counted. When the dataset's sequence differs from the model's, a domain is placed by its residues and marked not placed if they are not found once in the model.

"Carries a site" means a SUMO acceptor lysine of the dataset lies inside the domain's boundaries; it says nothing about how much of the protein is modified there.

Cartoon or space-filling

draw protein as → space-filling draws every atom of the model as a sphere of its van der Waals size, so you see the protein's surface and how exposed each lysine is. The site lysines are then painted onto the surface in their colour (the selected site slightly enlarged); the protein colourings (domains, Pfam, confidence) work in both styles. A SUMO2 drawn at a site follows the same style.

Room for SUMO2/3

For every lysine of the model, SUMOscape asks whether there is physical space next to it for a SUMO2/3 to sit. This is the calculation of the Ribosome SUMO Viewer (Bernhard Buss, ETH Zurich), ported unchanged, with the same placement set and classes; it runs in your browser when a protein is shown here (a few seconds for most proteins) and the progress is shown in the bar.

How. The folded core of SUMO2 (residues 17–88 of PDB 1WM3) is held rigid; the C-terminal tail (89–93 and the isopeptide bond) is treated as a straight tether of 6–19 Å from the lysine's NZ. 48 tether directions × 4 lengths × 96 orientations of the core give 18,432 placements. A placement is possible when the glycine bonded to the lysine (2.4 Å from NZ) has 3 Å of space, the tether line is free, and no backbone or Cβ atom of the SUMO core comes within 2.2 Å of an atom of the protein (on a 0.6 Å grid). The share of possible placements gives the class: ■ room ≥ 1 %, ■ tight 0.1–1 %, ■ no room < 0.1 %.

Controls. colour sites by → Room for SUMO colours the sites by class; show keeps only the sites with room (or room or tight); SUMO at draws the SUMO2 core (green) at a placement that fits — the tether direction nearest to the mean of all possible ones, the core turned away from the protein — at the selected site or at every site shown with room (up to 60). The bar counts the sites and the other lysines in each class; the site details give the share.

Read it with care. The test is lenient by design: only the SUMO backbone and Cβ are tested, so room means "a SUMO could fit", while no room is the firm statement — and only for this one rigid conformation. The model is the unmodified, monomeric AlphaFold prediction: partners, other conformations and flexible loops are not considered, low-confidence regions (often where SUMO sites are) are taken as placed, the tail cannot bend around obstacles, and the access of the conjugating enzyme UBC9 is not tested. The share of placements is not a probability.

What a predicted structure can and cannot say

AlphaFold models are predictions of the unmodified, monomeric protein. Low-confidence (pLDDT < 50) regions are usually disordered and should not be read as a fold; many SUMO sites lie in such regions. Nothing about SUMO itself, partners, or the conjugated state is in the model.

Modelling a SUMO conjugate (in preparation)

The details of a site offer input files for structure predictors in which SUMO2 is covalently attached to that lysine (isopeptide bond between SUMO2 Gly93 C and the lysine NZ): a Boltz-2 YAML file with a bond constraint and an AlphaFold 3 JSON with bondedAtomPairs. Run them with your own installation; the result can then be compared with the unmodified model. This is the first step of the planned conjugate-modelling module; see Roadmap.

The site table

The table lists the sites of the proteins shown (active protein first). Columns: site, the Z-score in each condition, the q-values of the conditions with a test, and the motif. A click on a column header sorts; a second click reverses. Filters: free text (gene or residue, e.g. K524), significance in one condition or in any, and motif.

Site details

The details give the protein, the residue with its sequence window, the motif, the annotation that covers the lysine (domain, Pfam, region), the Z-scores, fold changes and q-values per condition, the cell lines it was detected in, and what UniProt records at that lysine. Links open UniProt, the AlphaFold DB and PhosphoSitePlus; buttons show the site on the structure and download modelling input.

Datasets

Human cells — Hendriks IA, Lyon D, Su D, Skotte NH, Daniel JA, Jensen LJ, Nielsen ML. Site-specific characterization of endogenous SUMOylation across species and organs. Nat Commun 9:2456 (2018), Supplementary Data 1 (CC BY 4.0). sites on protein accessions, detected in HEK293, HeLa and U2OS and quantified in HEK293 under control, MG132 and heat shock: Z-scores, log2 fold changes and q-values as published.

Mouse organs — the same paper, Supplementary Data 5: sites on accessions in eight organs, with per-organ Z-scores, presence and an ANOVA q-value across organs.

Annotation — UniProtKB release (snapshot taken when the data were built, so results are reproducible), Pfam via the InterPro API. Structures — AlphaFold DB, fetched live.

Your data

Load a table… (or drop a file on the block) reads a CSV or TSV into the page as a further dataset. Nothing is uploaded or stored; it is gone when the page is closed.

  • protein (UniProt accession) or gene identifies the protein; position the lysine.
  • z_<condition> columns become one panel each (e.g. z_control, z_treated); optional q_<condition> and lfc_<condition> columns give significance and fold change.
  • Without z_ columns, every other numeric column becomes a panel of its own.

Proteins in the bundled annotation are drawn at once; others are fetched from UniProt. Download an example.

Exporting

SVG downloads the current view as a vector graphic — the site maps of all proteins shown, the landscape or the heatmap — ready for Illustrator or Inkscape. PNG downloads the same at twice the screen resolution. CSV downloads the sites of the table with all their values. The structure view has Mol*'s own screenshot button (camera icon at its top right).

Figure fonts

Aa Fonts, next to the download buttons, sets the text of the site map, the landscape and the heatmap — on screen and in the downloaded SVG and PNG alike, so what you see is what you get.

  • Font: Helvetica/Arial, Arial, Calibri, Verdana, Times New Roman, Georgia, Courier New or the system font. These are fonts most computers have, so an SVG opens with the same font elsewhere.
  • Site labels — the residue labels at the lollipop heads (K524) and the site names in the landscape and the heatmap.
  • Axis — tick numbers, axis titles, the residue letters when zoomed in, the numbers of the colour scale.
  • Chart labels — condition names, track names, domain and region labels, quadrant names, protein headers.

Each group has a size (5–30 px; one px is one pt when the SVG is placed at 100 %), B for bold and I for italic. Black text turns the grey labels black for print. Presets: Journal, 7 pt (Arial, black), Presentation, Poster; Reset returns to the screen default. Margins, track rows and label spacing grow with the text so that larger labels do not overlap. The setting is kept in this browser.

The Overview window

Counts of the dataset (sites, proteins, significant sites per condition, sites per quadrant of the landscape) and of the proteins shown (sites, sites in domains, motif share, significant sites). These are counts only — no tests are run in the page.

Overlooked in the earlier tools

What SUMOscape changes, and why:

  • Domains missing: the SUMO Site Map kept only seven UniProt feature types (topology, transmembrane, compositional bias, signal peptides, lysine modifications and all Pfam domains were absent) and hid every domain for isoform accessions.
  • Ratios hid the baseline: one lollipop per stress vs control; control itself was never drawn.
  • Z-scores misread: the per-site standardisation (the three Z-scores add up to zero) was not stated, so "high Z" could be read as "large change".
  • Significance as opacity: q-values were encoded as marker transparency, which is hard to read and collides with overlapping points; SUMOscape uses filled/open heads.
  • One protein at a time: no way to compare several proteins or add a list.
  • Ribosome only (Ribosome SUMO Viewer): structure, statistics and help built around one complex.
  • Single motif flag: only "KxE yes/no"; no ψKxE or inverted motif, nothing about competing lysine modifications.
  • Not citable / not versioned: no version, no changelog, annotation fetched without recording the UniProt release.
  • Server-bound (Shiny): needs a running R server; SUMOscape is a static page.

Limitations of SUMOscape

  • The site data are those of the published tables: no re-quantification, no peptide-level evidence, a single cell line (HEK293) for the quantification.
  • Z-scores carry no magnitude (see What a Z-score is here); fold changes are relative to control and capped by detection.
  • Feature mapping onto isoforms is by exact residues; features in alternatively spliced segments are left out.
  • AlphaFold models are of the canonical, unmodified monomer; isoform-only sites cannot be shown on a structure.
  • Pfam and AlphaFold depend on public services; the UniProt and Pfam snapshot of the build is used when they cannot be reached.
  • Statistics are descriptive counts only.
